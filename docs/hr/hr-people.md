## Part 1 — Compensation Philosophy

> "Comp clarity beats comp competitiveness. Pay fair + don't haggle."

### 1.1 Compensation Bands per Role × Level

All compensation is set by **band** before any offer is made. Bands are owned by the People function (or founder pre-People hire) and reviewed annually.

| Role family | Levels | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Engineering** | L3–L6 | L3: mid-level; L4: senior; L5: staff; L6: principal/distinguished |
| **AI/ML** | L3–L6 | Same ladder as Engineering; applied vs. research track split at L4 |
| **Design** | L3–L5 | L3: mid; L4: senior; L5: staff designer |
| **OSINT Analyst** | A2–A5 | A2: analyst; A3: senior analyst; A4: lead analyst; A5: principal analyst |
| **Sales** | S1–S4 | S1: SDR; S2: AE mid-market; S3: AE enterprise; S4: Director |
| **Customer Success** | CS1–CS3 | CS1: CSM; CS2: senior CSM; CS3: head of CS |
| **Ops / Security** | O2–O5 | O2: specialist; O3: senior specialist; O4: manager/lead; O5: head |
| **G&A** | G2–G4 | Finance, Legal, Ops generalist |

Bands contain: base salary range (min / mid / max) + equity range (option grant at hire, in shares and $ equivalent at current 409A).

Bands reviewed against benchmark data annually in Q4. Salary data sources: Levels.fyi (engineering), Radford / McLagan (broader), and regional equivalents (UA: DOU salary survey; PL: No Fluff Jobs data).

### 1.2 Benchmark: 60th–80th Percentile per Region

**Target:** 60th–80th percentile of total cash compensation (base + bonus, excluding equity) for the relevant region and level.

**Why this range:**
- 60th percentile: fair; attracts mission-aligned candidates who value equity + impact
- 80th percentile: competitive; used for hard-to-fill roles (senior AI/ML, CISO, senior engineers)
- Not 90th–100th: we cannot win a pure comp war; we win on mission + equity + autonomy

**Per-region adjustment:**
| Region | Relative to US-remote baseline |
|---|---|
| United States (remote) | 100% baseline |
| United Kingdom | 70–80% of US |
| EU (Germany, Poland) | 60–75% of US |
| Ukraine | 40–55% of US — but **generous by UA market standards**: target 80th–90th of UA market |
| Other regions | Case-by-case; Levels.fyi + local benchmarks |

UA-resident employees are paid in USD (or EUR on request) via Deel/Remote or a local Ukrainian entity, with a generous UA-market premium to reflect the mission-critical nature of UA-based talent and the operational risks they accept.

### 1.3 Equity Bands Aligned to Role × Level

Equity is granted as stock options (ISO for US employees; EMI / unapproved options for UK; phantom equity or warrants for others depending on jurisdiction).

| Level | Typical hire grant (% of fully diluted) |
|---|---|
| L3 / A2 / S1 | 0.05–0.10% |
| L4 / A3 / S2 | 0.10–0.25% |
| L5 / A4 / S3 | 0.25–0.50% |
| L6 / A5 / S4 | 0.50–1.00% |
| Director / Head | 0.75–1.50% |
| VP / C-suite | 1.00–3.00% |
| Founding-era key hire (first 10) | 0.50–1.50% |

All grants: 4-year vesting, 1-year cliff, 10-year exercise window (post-termination), monthly vesting after cliff.

409A current at all times — disclosed to candidates in $ equivalent so the % is meaningful.

### 1.4 No Equity-vs-Salary Tradeoff Trickery

We do not offer below-band salaries in exchange for above-band equity ("risk premium" framing). Equity is a bonus for shared success — not a substitute for fair compensation. Every candidate should be able to meet their cost of living from base salary alone.

### 1.5 Annual Compensation Review (Not Negotiation Theater)

Every November:
1. People function pulls benchmark data for all current roles and levels
2. Any band falling below the 60th percentile benchmark is adjusted upward
3. Individual employees below band mid are reviewed for market-rate adjustment
4. Adjustments effective January 1

**Process:** Manager + People → propose adjustment → exec approval → employee notification by December 15. No individual salary negotiation at review — adjustments are band-driven, not bargaining.

**Promotion adjustments:** Processed at the time of promotion, not at annual review.

### 1.6 Per-Region Cost-of-Labor Adjustments

Cost-of-labor is recalculated whenever a team member relocates to a new region or when a new hire is in a region not yet represented.

Rules:
- Relocation from higher-cost to lower-cost region: salary adjustments applied 6 months after relocation (not immediately — avoids penalizing short-term moves)
- Relocation from lower-cost to higher-cost region: salary adjusted upward immediately
- UA-resident employees: no downward adjustment regardless of cost-of-living changes during the conflict period (explicit policy)

### 1.7 Transparent Internal Band Visibility

All employees can see the full band for their level and the band for the level above. This is shared at onboarding and available in the internal HR handbook.

Bands are **not** shared externally on job postings initially (until required by law, e.g., Colorado, New York, California for US roles). However, every job posting states a range once in the offer stage.

### 1.8 No-Haggle Offers

Offers are made at a specific number within the band — not at the floor of a range. The number is chosen based on:
- Candidate's current market rate (if known + relevant)
- Where in the band their skills and experience place them
- Team equity/salary context (avoid internal pay compression)

Once the number is set, it is defended with data, not negotiated up. Candidates who request increases above the band mid-point are told: "This is our band for this role; we can revisit at your 6-month review if performance warrants."

Counter-offers from other companies: we consider matching if the offer is within our band; we do not match outside-band offers.

### 1.9 Annual Refresh Equity Grants

Each December, top performers (top 20% by manager + peer assessment) receive a refresh equity grant:
- L3–L4: 25–50% of initial hire grant size
- L5+: 50–100% of initial hire grant size

Refresh grants have a 4-year vest, no cliff (immediate monthly vesting from grant date).

Purpose: reward retention + prevent cliff-based departures. Not a surprise — communicated in the annual review conversation.

## Part 2 — Remote / Hybrid / Travel Policy

> "UA team reality includes power + sirens. Build flex in."

### 2.1 Default: Remote-First

Aegis Lens is a **remote-first** company. This means:
- No physical office is required to do your job
- In-person time is intentional and planned, not mandatory by default
- All tools, processes, and communications assume async-first
- Time zones are respected; meetings are documented

Remote-first ≠ remote-only. We hold intentional in-person moments (see §2.4 and §2.5).

### 2.2 Time-Zone Overlap Rules

**Team overlap requirement:** Each team must have at least **4 hours of overlapping working hours** per business day across all team members.

**Core hours:** 10:00–14:00 UTC is the default core window for multi-team meetings and synchronous collaboration. Teams may set their own core windows as long as there is 4h overlap across the team.

**Policy rules:**
- Hiring: new hires must be within a zone that enables 4h overlap with their direct team
- Async-first: no meeting required for information that can be conveyed in writing; document before meeting
- No meeting > 90 minutes (prefer two 45-min meetings)
- Meeting recordings: all team meetings recorded (with consent) and shared in the team's shared drive within 4h

**UA-specific:** Ukrainian team members operating under air raid alerts may miss scheduled calls without notice. All critical decisions must be async-accessible within 24h of any call.

### 2.3 Quarterly Team Offsites

Each team holds a **quarterly offsite** — 2–3 days in-person, company-covered:

| Element | Spec |
|---|---|
| Duration | 2–3 days |
| Location | Chosen by the team; avoid high-cost cities unless there's strong reason |
| Coverage | Flights, accommodation, meals during offsite days |
| Agenda | 60% work (roadmap, retrospective, design sessions), 40% social (team bonding, local activities) |
| Planning lead | Team lead, minimum 6 weeks in advance |
| Budget | $1,500 per person per offsite (all-in) |

UA-based team members: if travel to the offsite location is not safe or feasible, a hybrid format is offered (remote participation for those who cannot travel + async pre-work).

### 2.4 Annual All-Hands Offsite

Full company all-hands, once per year:

| Element | Spec |
|---|---|
| Duration | 3–4 days |
| Timing | Q3 (after Series A; calendar year mid-point) |
| Location | Neutral city with good connectivity (Berlin, Warsaw, Vilnius target year 1–2) |
| Coverage | Flights, accommodation, all meals |
| Agenda | Day 1: retrospective + culture; Day 2–3: cross-team roadmap; Day 4: team activities + celebration |
| Budget | $2,500 per person all-in |
| UA team | Travel facilitated by company; if travel not feasible, hybrid participation + async content access |

### 2.5 Travel + Expense Policy

**Business travel categories:**

| Category | Coverage | Approval |
|---|---|---|
| Team offsite | Full (§2.3) | Team lead |
| All-hands | Full (§2.4) | Automatic |
| Conference / event | Per event budget (see `docs/events/events.md §1.3`) | Manager |
| Sales / customer travel | Full; reasonable | Manager |
| Personal relocation | Not covered (except first-month setup support for senior hires) | N/A |

**Expense rules:**
- Economy class for flights < 6 hours; business class approved for > 6 hours + management-level
- Hotels: reasonably priced for the city; no 5-star unless city has no mid-range equivalent
- Meals: up to $80/person/day during business travel; $50/person/day during offsites
- Submit receipts within 30 days via expense tool (Expensify / Ramp)
- No cash reimbursements > $50 (requires receipt)

### 2.6 Equipment + Home-Office Stipend

**At hire:**
- **Laptop:** Company-issued MacBook Pro (M-series, 14" for individual contributors, 16" for engineers / heavy users). Returned at departure.
- **Home-office setup:** $1,000 one-time stipend (desk, monitor, keyboard, webcam, headset). Receipts required; no return obligation after 2 years.

**Ongoing:**
- **Internet:** Up to $80/month internet stipend (reimbursed monthly)
- **Learning budget:** $1,500/year per person (books, courses, conferences; pre-approval for > $300/item)

### 2.7 Coworking Budget

Employees who prefer not to work from home may expense a coworking membership:
- Up to $200/month in high-cost cities (SF, London, Zurich)
- Up to $100/month in mid-cost cities (Berlin, Warsaw, Amsterdam)
- Up to $50/month in lower-cost cities / remote regions

No approval needed; submit monthly via expense tool. If a team cluster (3+ people) wants a dedicated desk or private office in the same city, company will consider shared office space on a case-by-case basis.

### 2.8 UA-Specific: Power-Outage + Crisis Allowance

Ukrainian team members have additional support:

| Situation | Company support |
|---|---|
| Power outages (scheduled + emergency) | Starlink hardware kit provided (company-owned, loaned) if needed; generator fuel expense $75/month |
| Air-raid disruption | Any missed meeting or work-hour gap due to air raid is automatically excused — no PTO deduction |
| Evacuation / relocation | 2 weeks paid evacuation leave + $500 logistics support if forced to relocate within UA or internationally |
| Mental health impact | Additional 3 paid wellbeing days per year (see Part 4) |

### 2.9 Wellbeing Days (Separate from PTO)

In addition to PTO (see Part 4), every employee receives **6 wellbeing days per year** — separate from vacation and sick leave:
- Purpose: mental health recovery, avoiding burnout, unstructured rest
- No approval required; notify manager same day or prior day
- Not accumulated year-to-year (use it or lose it — wellbeing days are not hoarded)
- UA team: additional 3 wellbeing days (total 9) acknowledging operational stress of the conflict context

## Part 3 — Diversity, Equity, Inclusion

> "Structure > slogans. Audit + adjust > performative posts."

### 3.1 Sourcing Pipeline Diversity Targets (Top of Funnel)

Targeted representation in candidate pipelines before any selection occurs:

| Dimension | Pipeline target |
|---|---|
| Women / non-binary | ≥ 50% of screened candidates for all roles |
| Ukrainian / Eastern European | ≥ 40% for OSINT analyst and product roles |
| Candidates from non-English primary background | ≥ 30% for all roles |
| Age diversity | Avoid clustering; mix of early-career and experienced |

**Structural sourcing actions:**
- Post to women-in-tech communities (Women Who Code, Girls in Tech, #WomenInOSINT channels)
- Partner with Ukrainian tech communities (Dou.ua, Projector, Women Who Code Kyiv chapter)
- Sponsor scholarships / fellowships in journalism + security programs to build a pipeline for future hires
- Job descriptions: run through Textio or manual review for gender-coded language before every post

### 3.2 Structured Interviews (Lower Bias)

Every interview loop uses:
- A written scorecard with defined criteria (no "culture fit" as a criterion — see §3.4)
- The same questions asked to all candidates for a given role (documented in the interview kit)
- No ad-hoc questions added by individual interviewers
- Debrief: each interviewer submits scorecard before the group debrief — no anchoring to the first opinion heard

### 3.3 Per-Loop Demographic Balance (No Single-Demographic Panels)

Every interview panel of 3+ people must include:
- At least one woman or non-binary interviewer (where the team has one available)
- No all-founder panel for junior candidates (power imbalance)

If team makeup prevents this for a loop, the People team flags it and seeks a guest interviewer from another team or from our advisor network.

### 3.4 Pay-Equity Annual Audit

Every November (same time as comp review):
1. Pull salary data for all employees by level
2. Run regression: does gender, nationality, or ethnicity predict salary within a level? (control for tenure and hire date)
3. Any statistically significant gap > 5%: adjust the lower-paid group upward immediately
4. Results shared in aggregate with the full team (no individual data)

**No "culture fit" as a comp criterion.** Comp is set by band + performance, not subjective fit assessments which correlate with demographic bias.

### 3.5 Promotion-Rate Parity Tracking

Quarterly: compare promotion rates across demographic groups. If any group is promoted at < 80% the rate of the highest-promoted group (same tenure cohort), flag for investigation.

Common causes: recency bias in performance review, visibility gap (remote employees less visible than in-person), pipeline gap (fewer women in senior candidate pool due to earlier-career representation issues).

### 3.6 Structural Changes Over Mandatory Training

We do not run mandatory DEI workshops or sensitivity training as a primary intervention. Research shows limited long-term impact and can create backlash. Instead, we focus on structural changes:
- Structured interviews (§3.2)
- Blind screening for junior roles (name + university redacted)
- Salary bands + no-haggle offers (§1.8)
- Promotion-rate tracking (§3.5)
- Pay-equity audit (§3.4)

Optional DEI learning resources are available and encouraged, not mandated.

### 3.7 Inclusive Language Guide (Operational, Not Preachy)

A short living document (max 2 pages) covering:
- Preferred terminology for the conflict context (avoid terms that could imply political bias)
- Role descriptions: use gender-neutral job titles ("analyst" not "analyst/analyst")
- Communications: "everyone" / "all" over "guys"; "they/them" as default singular where gender unknown
- What to do when a colleague uses non-inclusive language: direct, private, non-punitive feedback

Updated when issues arise; not a comprehensive manual.

### 3.8 Annual Internal DEI Report

Published internally to all staff every Q4:
- Pipeline diversity metrics (top of funnel vs. hired)
- Pay-equity audit results (aggregate)
- Promotion-rate parity results
- Any structural changes made this year
- 3 specific goals for next year (measurable)

Not published externally in Year 1 (small team, data unreliable with small N). External publication considered at > 50 employees.

## Part 4 — Wellbeing & Mental Health

> "Burnout in this domain is fast + invisible. Engineer prevention."

### 4.1 Mandatory Wellbeing Days (Separate from PTO)

6 wellbeing days per year (UA team: 9). See `docs/hr/hr-people.md §2.9` for details.

Additionally: unlimited sick leave (no cap, no approval required for < 5 consecutive days; doctor's note for > 5 days).

### 4.2 Mental Health Benefit

Every employee has access to a mental health platform — available in their region:

| Region | Provider | Coverage |
|---|---|---|
| US | Spring Health or Lyra Health | Up to 16 sessions/year; EAP included |
| UK | Spill or BUPA mental health | 8–12 sessions/year |
| EU | regional equivalent | Minimum 6 sessions/year covered |
| UA | Ukrainian platform (Rozmova, Tobi OK) + English-language option | 8 sessions/year + crisis hotline access |
| Other | $200/year mental health spending account (therapist of choice) | Annual |

Enrollment is voluntary and completely confidential — the company sees only aggregate utilization, not individual names or session content.

### 4.3 Per-Role Exposure-Rotation Policy (Analysts, Moderators)

Staff in roles that regularly handle traumatic content (graphic imagery, casualty reports, disturbing footage) are protected by:

- **Maximum direct exposure:** No more than 4 hours of graphic-content review in a single shift (then mandatory break or task switch)
- **Rotation:** Content-heavy tasks rotated across the analyst/moderation team on a weekly cycle — no one person handles the worst content exclusively
- **Buddy system:** During any session reviewing graphic content, a second person is available to check in (can be remote/async; purpose is not being alone with distressing content)
- **Opt-out:** Any team member can temporarily opt out of graphic-content queues without penalty, question, or performance impact
- **Track:** Managers log (privately) any instance of distress response; pattern triggers a mandatory check-in and potential temporary role adjustment

### 4.4 Content-Warning + Blur Defaults in Workspace

Product defaults for internal team use:
- Graphic imagery: blurred by default in the event detail panel; click to reveal
- Content warnings: auto-generated tag on events classified as graphic (casualty imagery, graphic violence)
- User setting: can disable blur for their own session (not possible to disable team-wide without manager approval)
- API / data exports: raw imagery flagged with `content_warning: true` header field

### 4.5 Quarterly Wellbeing Check-Ins (Not Performance)

Every manager conducts a dedicated 30-minute wellbeing check-in with each direct report each quarter. This is **separate from performance reviews**:
- Not recorded or summarized in HR systems
- Manager listens; does not evaluate
- Standard questions: "How is your energy level lately? What's creating the most friction? Is there anything the company could do differently?"
- Manager can share themes (anonymized) with People team for systemic issues

### 4.6 Trauma-Informed Practices in Code of Conduct and Team Culture

The team code of conduct (internal) includes:
- Acknowledgment that our work exposes people to content depicting real human suffering — this is not routine
- Explicit norm: it is okay to say "I need to step away from this content right now"
- No bravado culture around exposure ("I can handle anything")
- Debrief culture: after a major incident review, the team does a brief (10-min) human check-in before returning to work

### 4.7 On-Call Limits: No More Than 2× / Month per Person

Engineering on-call rotation:
- Maximum 2 on-call weeks per calendar month per engineer
- On-call shifts: 7 days, with a 48-hour recovery buffer before the next shift
- Incidents that extend past 2 hours trigger automatic secondary responder
- On-call compensation: $150 flat/week (on-call stipend) + compensatory time for incidents > 2h at night/weekend

### 4.8 No-Meeting Fridays

Fridays are meeting-free by default for the full company:
- No internally-scheduled calls or standups on Fridays
- Exception: customer calls if the customer initiates (unavoidable)
- Fridays used for: deep work, learning, maintenance, wellbeing
- Managers lead by example — no Slack pings expecting responses on Fridays (async queue)

### 4.9 Sabbatical Eligibility (4 Weeks after 4 Years)

After 4 years of continuous employment, every employee earns a sabbatical:
- Duration: 4 weeks of additional paid leave (beyond their annual PTO)
- Can be taken in one block or two 2-week blocks
- Eligible again after every 4 years thereafter
- Purpose: travel, rest, creative projects, personal development — no work obligations
- Rollover: sabbatical can be postponed up to 1 year if operational needs require it (manager + employee mutual agreement)

### 4.10 Crisis Access: External Therapist Directory

Available to all employees: a vetted list of therapists specializing in:
- Work-related trauma (vicarious trauma, conflict content exposure)
- Ukrainian-speaking therapists (for UA-based staff)
- Remote / online therapy (for distributed team)

Directory maintained by the People team, updated annually. Company covers sessions up to the limits in §4.2; beyond that, employees can use the mental health spending account.

### 4.11 Annual Wellbeing Pulse Survey

Every October, a 10-question anonymous survey:
1. Overall energy / burnout level (1–10)
2. Confidence that the company cares about your wellbeing (1–10)
3. Manager wellbeing check-in usefulness (1–10)
4. Content exposure comfort level (for analysts/moderation) (1–10)
5. On-call burden (for engineers) (1–10)
6. Work/life balance (1–10)
7. Top stressor at work (open text)
8. One thing the company could do better for your wellbeing (open text)
9. Would you recommend Aegis Lens as a place to work? (NPS 0–10)
10. Anything else? (open text)

Results shared with the full team in aggregate within 30 days. Top 3 action items identified and owners assigned.

## Part 5 — Security & OpSec Training

> "Our staff is a target. Train accordingly."

### 5.1 Day-1 Security Training (Auth, Phishing, Secrets)

Every new employee completes the following on their first day before accessing any company systems:

**Module 1 — Authentication hygiene (30 min):**
- MFA required on all company accounts (Google Workspace, GitHub, AWS, Cloudflare) — no exceptions
- Passkey enrollment where available
- Yubikey hardware key provided to all engineers, analysts, and senior staff (see §5.9)
- Password manager setup (1Password teams, company-licensed)
- No password reuse policy

**Module 2 — Phishing recognition (30 min):**
- How to identify spear-phishing attempts (especially relevant — we are a target for state actors)
- What to do on receiving a suspicious email: report to security@aegislens.io; do not click
- Social engineering: never share credentials, even with "IT support"
- Vishing (voice phishing): how to verify requests

**Module 3 — Secrets management (20 min):**
- Never commit credentials to GitHub (enforced by gitleaks pre-commit hook)
- Env vars via approved secrets manager (AWS Secrets Manager / Doppler) — never in `.env` files in repos
- What to do if a secret is accidentally exposed: immediately rotate + notify security team
- No sharing of API keys via Slack/email — use 1Password sharing or secrets manager

### 5.2 Annual Security Refresh

Every September: updated 1-hour training covering:
- Threat landscape changes (new attacker TTPs relevant to intelligence platforms)
- Incidents from the past year (de-identified, what we learned)
- Updated phishing simulation results + discussion
- Policy changes + new tools

Completion tracked in HR system. Required before annual access review.

### 5.3 OpSec Training for Analysts (Handling Sensitive Sources + Data)

Delivered in Week 2 of onboarding for all OSINT analysts:

**Module A — Source protection (45 min):**
- Never reveal the identity of a source, even internally, without the source's explicit consent
- How to store source contact information (encrypted notes only, never in plain-text Slack)
- Signal / encrypted messaging for source communication
- What to do if you suspect a source has been compromised

**Module B — Data handling (30 min):**
- PII redaction rules (names, faces, locations of civilians must not be amplified)
- What data can be publicly cited vs. internal-only (need-to-know)
- Data classification system (Public / Internal / Restricted / Critical)
- Never screenshot classified events to personal devices

**Module C — Digital OpSec for analysts (45 min):**
- Separate browser profiles for research (never mix personal + professional research identity)
- VPN usage rules for sensitive source research
- Google dorking and open-source research from a sterile environment
- Sock puppet policy: no analyst creates fake personas without written security team approval

### 5.4 Travel-Security Protocol (UA + Risky Regions)

All employees traveling to Ukraine, conflict-adjacent countries, or other elevated-risk regions:

**Pre-travel:**
- Notify security@aegislens.io at least 7 days before travel
- Receive country-specific threat briefing (current alerts, border crossing rules, comms infrastructure)
- Declutter devices (remove sensitive data, use a travel-only device if possible)
- Share emergency contact + itinerary with a designated colleague

**During travel:**
- Daily check-in protocol (quick Telegram ping to designated contact at a set time)
- If check-in missed: security team notified and begins reach-out protocol
- UA-specific: register with CERT-UA emergency contact if in a high-risk region
- No work on public Wi-Fi without VPN active

**Post-travel:**
- Device wipe and restore from clean backup if traveling to adversarial environments
- Debrief with security team within 48h if any suspicious contact or incident occurred

### 5.5 Personal-Device Hardening Guide

Delivered as a self-service guide at onboarding; enforced for engineers and analysts:

| Device | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Laptop (company-issued) | Full-disk encryption (FileVault on Mac), auto-lock 2 min, no local admin for daily use account |
| Mobile (personal, if used for work) | Screen lock + biometric; work apps in a separate profile (Android) or MDM-enrolled (iOS) |
| Home network | Change default router password; WPA3 if available; IoT devices on a separate VLAN |
| Public Wi-Fi | VPN required for any work activity |

Company-issued devices are enrolled in MDM (Jamf for Mac) — allows remote wipe if device is lost or stolen. Employee notified before any remote action (except in case of confirmed theft).

### 5.6 Social-Media OpSec (Especially for Analysts)

**For all staff:**
- Do not post about internal Aegis Lens capabilities, current investigations, or source networks on social media
- Do not list internal systems, data sources, or infrastructure details on LinkedIn
- "Works at Aegis Lens" is fine; specific role descriptions that reveal capabilities should be reviewed with security before posting

**For analysts specifically:**
- Recommended: maintain a professional OSINT persona separate from personal identity
- Do not post evidence of active geolocation work until the work is published or explicitly cleared
- Do not reveal the existence of a specific source relationship (even vaguely) — adversaries aggregate signals
- If an analyst's identity becomes known in the OSINT community, review their exposure and consider persona compartmentalization

### 5.7 Phishing Simulations (Quarterly)

The security team runs simulated phishing campaigns quarterly:
- 3 difficulty levels: generic scam (easiest), spear-phish using public info (medium), highly personalized (hardest — only for senior staff)
- If a team member clicks: they immediately receive a brief educational popup + their manager is notified (for support, not discipline)
- Results reported in aggregate to all staff
- No "naming and shaming"; chronic clickers (3+ consecutive quarters) receive 1-on-1 coaching

### 5.8 Incident-Response Basic Training (All Staff)

1-hour module in Month 2 of onboarding, repeated at the annual refresh:
- What constitutes a security incident (account compromise, data breach, unauthorized access, lost device)
- How to report: security@aegislens.io + Slack `#security-incidents` (24h monitored)
- What to do while waiting for the security team: don't delete, don't reuse credentials, don't tell the suspected attacker you've noticed
- Incident categories + response SLAs (per `docs/security/security-incident-runbook.md`)

### 5.9 Mandatory MFA + Passkeys + Yubikeys for Engineers / Staff

**Requirements:**
| Role | MFA requirement |
|---|---|
| All staff | App-based TOTP (Google Authenticator, Authy) OR passkey on all company accounts — SMS-MFA NOT accepted (SIM-swap risk) |
| Engineers | Yubikey 5 NFC (company-provided) required for GitHub, AWS, production systems |
| Analysts | Yubikey required for source-sensitive data systems |
| Senior leadership | Yubikey required for all accounts |

Yubikeys are provisioned at onboarding. Lost Yubikeys: report within 4h; remote de-authorization. Replacement within 48h at company expense.

### 5.10 Per-Role Advanced Training

In addition to baseline training:

| Role | Advanced training | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| OSINT Analyst | Vicarious trauma awareness + self-care (60 min, certified facilitator); digital counter-surveillance | Month 1 + annual |
| Engineers | OWASP Top 10 for our specific stack; threat modeling workshop; secure code review | Quarterly |
| Moderation team | Crisis moderation under stress; escalation protocols; legal exposure awareness | Quarterly |
| Sales / CS | Enterprise customer security requirements; how to answer security questionnaires honestly | At first enterprise deal |
| Senior leadership | Board-level threat briefing; crisis comms; social engineering at executive level | Semi-annually |
| Journalists & external collaborators | Basic OpSec for external contributors (separate 30-min brief sent before any collaboration) | Per engagement |
