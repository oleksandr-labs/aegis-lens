# Investor NDA (rarely used)

> **Read this first.** Most professional investors (VCs, angels, funds) will
> **decline** to sign an NDA before or during early diligence. This is normal,
> industry-standard behavior — it is **not** a red flag, and asking a brand-name
> fund to sign one before a first meeting signals inexperience. Founders should
> generally **not** request investor NDAs at the pitch stage.
>
> Use this template only in the narrow cases where an NDA is genuinely
> warranted and the investor has agreed in principle:
>
> - Late-stage diligence involving disclosure of **specific** trade secrets
>   (e.g., proprietary verification algorithms, source relationships) beyond the
>   pitch deck and data room.
> - A strategic / corporate investor whose core business **directly competes**
>   with ours.
> - Disclosure of regulated or third-party-confidential data we are contractually
>   bound to protect.

**INVESTOR NON-DISCLOSURE AGREEMENT**

Between `[AEGIS_ENTITY_NAME]` (the "**Company**") and `[INVESTOR_NAME]` (the
"**Investor**"), effective `[EFFECTIVE_DATE]`.

### 1. Purpose

The Investor wishes to evaluate a potential investment in the Company (the
"**Purpose**") and will receive certain Confidential Information for that
limited purpose.

### 2. Confidential Information; Exclusions

As defined in the Company's standard NDA, with the standard four exclusions.
**Carve-out for portfolio activity:** the Investor's review of, or investment in,
other companies in the same sector is not a breach, provided the Investor does
not use or disclose the Company's Confidential Information. The Investor's
mental impressions and general industry knowledge ("residuals") are not
restricted.

### 3. Obligations

Use solely for the Purpose; reasonable care; disclosure limited to the
Investor's partners, employees, and professional advisors with a need to know,
who are bound by confidentiality.

### 4. No Standstill; No Exclusivity

This Agreement imposes no standstill, no exclusivity, and no obligation to
invest. The Company may speak with other investors freely.

### 5. Term

`[TERM — default: eighteen (18) months]`, reflecting that investment-evaluation
confidentiality is shorter-lived. Trade-secret obligations survive for as long
as the information remains a trade secret.

### 6. Governing Law; Miscellaneous

Governed by `[GOVERNING_LAW]`; venue `[VENUE]`. Entire agreement; written
amendments; counterparts and electronic signature permitted.

**Redline notes (internal).** Expect heavy pushback. Investors will strike: any
standstill, broad definitions sweeping in residuals/portfolio activity (we
already carve these out in §2 — point to it), long terms (18mo is already
generous), and non-compete-flavored language. If an investor refuses outright,
do **not** treat it as a deal-breaker — proceed with a redacted data room
instead and disclose the crown-jewel material only at term-sheet stage.
